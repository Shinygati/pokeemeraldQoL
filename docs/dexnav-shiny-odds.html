How the calculator matches the game

The ordinary and boosted thresholds use the same integer rounding and 32-bit fixed-point operations as GetDexNavShinyThreshold in src/dexnav.c. Displayed “1 in” values are rounded. Automatic mode averages the two thresholds using the boost probability.

DexNav uses the original ORAS formula. At Search Level 0 the base rate is 1/4096 without the Charm, or three independent checks with it. Search Level contributes six units per level up to 100, two per level from 101–200, and one per level afterward; the total is divided by 100 and rounded up. Each DexNav check succeeds against that target out of 10,000.

DexNav gets one check without the Charm or three with it, plus four on boosted targets. The 50th encounter (displayed chain 49 before battle) gets five additional checks. At displayed chain 99 or 100, every roll permanently gets the 100th encounter's ten additional checks plus the four boosted shiny checks. There is no 5/8 reduction or linear chain scaling.

The ! marker's level and IV bonus remains separate: every fifth upcoming encounter is guaranteed boosted, and other encounters have about a 4% boost chance. At chain 99+, the maximum shiny odds apply even when the target does not show !. “Ordinary” and “Boosted” therefore show the same shiny chance at chain 99+.

These odds apply to an eligible shiny roll after the habitat, encounter-find and patch checks. Walking steps can produce no roll. Legal Pokémon generation must also succeed before a target appears. Probabilities do not guarantee a shiny within a particular number of attempts.

At chain 100, running from battle preserves the chain. Catching a shiny, encountering another DexNav species or changing maps still resets it. Shiny DexNav targets do not time out or flee from movement or distance.
